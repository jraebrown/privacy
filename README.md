# Personal privacy statement

## Plain-language summary

I have ADHD, which can affect how I process, remember, and recall spoken information. To help me stay present, understand conversations accurately, and follow through on commitments, I may use notes, reminders, personal reflections, voice notes, or speech-to-text tools.

I treat personal information respectfully and confidentially. I do not voluntarily disclose identifiable information about low-level, non-violent unlawful activity, such as personal drug use, unless there is a credible risk of serious harm or a legal or safeguarding reason to share it.

I only record or live-transcribe conversations with informed consent. Confidentiality has limits: I may share the minimum necessary information where someone may be at serious risk, where safeguarding is needed, or where the law requires it.

This is a personal ethical statement. It does not create legal privilege, a professional relationship, or obligations for other people or organisations.

## Personal statement: privacy, confidentiality, accessibility and harm reduction

### 1. Purpose and scope

This statement explains how I approach privacy, confidentiality, accessibility, note-taking, and information shared during personal conversations, peer support, community outreach, and informal community settings.

It reflects my personal ethical practice and accessibility needs. It does not create a counselling, psychotherapy, healthcare, legal, research, supervisory, educational, or professional relationship.

Where I act in a professional, placement, clinical, healthcare, research, or other regulated role, applicable professional standards, organisational policies, safeguarding procedures, and legal requirements take precedence over this statement.

### 2. Accessibility, assistive tools and speech-to-text

I have ADHD, which can affect how efficiently I absorb, organise, retain, and recall spoken information. To support communication, understanding, and follow-through, I may use structured notes, reminders, personal reflections, voice notes, or assistive speech-to-text tools.

These tools help me:

- Stay present during conversations.
- Remember commitments accurately.
- Reduce misunderstandings.
- Reflect on important discussions.
- Support learning and personal development.

These practices are intended to support accessibility and accuracy. They are not intended for surveillance, monitoring, or control.

**Live recording and real-time transcription**

I will only use live audio recording or real-time transcription in community outreach, peer-support, private group, or professional settings where I have informed, freely given consent from everyone present.

Before recording begins, I will aim to explain:

- What is being recorded or transcribed.
- Why it is being used.
- Where the information will be stored.
- How long it will be retained.
- Who can access it.
- How someone can withdraw consent.

I will stop recording or transcription if anyone withdraws consent. I will not use recordings or transcripts for research, training, publication, social media, or sharing with others without separate explicit permission.

**Data minimisation and transcripts**

Where live assistive transcription has been agreed, I will keep only what is needed for accessibility, safety, practical follow-up, or agreed actions. I will remove or reduce identifying details where possible.

Raw audio will be deleted as soon as the necessary accessible notes have been made, and in any event within 24 hours, unless there is a specific safety, legal, or organisational reason to retain it for longer.

**Post-conversation reflection**

Where live recording is inappropriate, unavailable, or consent has not been given, I will rely on personal written summaries or private voice memos recorded after the interaction has ended.

### 3. Privacy, confidentiality and harm reduction

I respect the privacy of people who speak with me. Information shared with me in confidence is treated as private and is not voluntarily shared, forwarded, or used for gossip, entertainment, or personal advantage.

In community and outreach settings, my approach is guided by harm reduction, dignity, respect, and concern for people’s wellbeing. I do not voluntarily disclose identifiable information about low-level, non-violent unlawful activity, such as personal drug use or possession, where there is no credible safeguarding concern or serious risk of harm.

I may use non-identifying, generalised information to support harm-reduction education, peer training, public health, or community safety. I will only do this where the information cannot reasonably be traced back to an identifiable person.

### 4. Safeguarding and limits of confidentiality

Confidentiality is important for building trust, but it is not absolute.

I may share information with appropriate emergency services, safeguarding bodies, or other relevant authorities where I reasonably believe disclosure is necessary to:

- Prevent serious physical harm or death.
- Protect a child or adult at risk.
- Respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation, coercion, or non-consensual acts.
- Comply with a legal obligation, court order, or lawful request.

Where disclosure is necessary, I aim to act proportionately and share only the minimum information required to address the immediate risk.

Where possible and appropriate, I will be transparent about the limits of confidentiality before a conversation begins. I will also seek consent before sharing information unless doing so would increase risk, be inappropriate, or is not required by law.

I do not promise that a person’s identity can always be protected. Where disclosure is necessary, I will consider confidentiality carefully and share only what is reasonably necessary, but I cannot guarantee anonymity.

### 5. Information security and retention

Where I keep notes or records, I aim to keep them proportionate, relevant, and secure. I focus on safety needs, practical actions, agreed follow-ups, and accessibility needs rather than detailed accounts of people’s private lives.

Digital records and reflections are stored with reasonable technical and organisational safeguards, including access controls and encryption where practicable.

I aim not to keep more information than is reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it was recorded. Information is securely deleted once it no longer serves an active support, accessibility, safety, or legal purpose.

Where records relate to identifiable individuals, I aim to apply UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 principles proportionately, including lawfulness, fairness, transparency, data minimisation, and security. Where I am acting in a formal or organisational role, the relevant policies and legal requirements take precedence.

### 6. Formal limits of this statement

This document is an expression of personal ethical practice. It does not create legal privilege, contractual rights, or obligations for third parties, including law enforcement.

It does not create a formal counselling, psychotherapy, legal, medical, supervisory, or therapeutic relationship. Formal services operate under separate, explicit written agreements and their own professional, legal, and organisational requirements.
